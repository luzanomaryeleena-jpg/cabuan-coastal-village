About the Project

Overview

Cabuan Coastal Village is a protected marine sanctuary in Guinsiliban, Camiguin — known for crystal-clear waters, rich marine biodiversity, and scenic coastal views. This project proposes the development of eco-friendly facilities: Eco Pods, Cottages, and Seafood Restaurant — built with sustainable materials like bamboo and nipa, designed to minimize environmental impact while offering quality tourism services.

Currently, the area has limited organized facilities. This plan develops it into a sustainable destination while strictly following DENR, LGU, and sanctuary regulations — balancing environmental protection, economic growth, and community participation.

Mission

Develop a sustainable tourism destination that protects the marine ecosystem, provides livelihood to locals, and offers unique nature-based experiences to visitors.

Compliance

All facilities and activities follow marine sanctuary rules. Harmful activities are prohibited; only low-impact tourism is allowed.

Objectives

  • Determine visitor preferences and community perception
  • Design eco-friendly facilities: Eco Pods, Cottages, Seafood Restaurant
  • Promote responsible tourism and environmental conservation
  • Generate employment and livelihood for local residents
  • Increase recognition of Cabuan as a premier eco-destination

Facilities & Services

Eco Pods

Small-scale, eco-friendly accommodation for overnight stay. Made of bamboo & nipa — natural, ventilated, ocean-view, low environmental impact.

Rate: ₱500 – ₱1,000 / night (Good for 2–3 pax)

Cottages

Open/semi-open shelters for day-use visitors. Resting, family bonding, sightseeing. Built with sustainable local materials, safe and comfortable.

Rate: ₱200 – ₱400 / day

Seafood Restaurant

Coastal dining with fresh & grilled seafood — fish, shrimp, squid, Filipino dishes, desserts. Supports local fishermen. Ocean view & relaxing ambiance.

Meal: ₱150 – ₱300 / serving

Other Services

  • ✅ Clean restrooms & shower facilities
  • ✅ Designated & regulated bonfire areas (with safety pits)
  • ✅ Proper waste management & segregation
  • ✅ Safety, security & lifeguard services
  • ✅ Environmental education guides
  • ✅ Coastal clean-up programs

Tourism Activities

✅ ALLOWED ACTIVITIES

  • • Swimming (designated zones only)
  • • Snorkeling & Diving (view Giant Clams & corals)
  • • Sightseeing & nature appreciation
  • • Family & friends bonding
  • • Photography & videography
  • • Regulated Bonfire Night — supervised, with fire pits & protection

❌ NOT ALLOWED

  • • Kayaking & boating (risk to marine life)
  • • Catch-and-cook fishing / any fishing
  • • Coral, shell or marine life collection
  • • Littering / improper waste disposal
  • • Open fires outside designated areas
  • • Any activity damaging the ecosystem

Visitor Guidelines

  • Follow all sanctuary rules and staff instructions
  • Respect marine life and natural surroundings
  • Stay only in designated visitor areas
  • Observe "Leave No Trace" policy
  • Environmental fee: ₱50 – ₱100 per person
  • Bonfire fee: ₱150 – ₱250

Nearby Attractions & Culture

🐚 Giant Clams Sanctuary

Protected area with giant clams up to 1m wide. Best for snorkeling — vibrant marine life & coral gardens. Managed by LGU to prevent overfishing & destruction.

⛰️ Mt. Ilihan

1,495m stratovolcano — one of Camiguin’s highest peaks. Hiking, scenic views of Bohol & Misamis, tropical forest trails, pilgrimage site during Lent.

🏛️ Moro Watchtower

16th-century historical landmark (12m tall) made of coral stones & lime. Built during Spanish era — symbol of Guinsiliban’s history & heritage.

🥥Local Culture

Traditional coconut sugar making, fishing heritage, warm hospitality. Authentic Camiguin culture and community life.

🎊 Festivals & Events

Town & barangay fiestas, summer sports leagues, cultural celebrations year-round. Vibrant community traditions.
